INTENDED CHANGE IN UNDERSTANDING
Starting intuition: real edits and passing checks make a completion report credible.
Intended understanding: those actions can be accurately reported while the completion claim exceeds their scope. Inspect both the evidence and its applicability. A verifier must also avoid treating missing evidence as proof of a false claim.
Practical consequence: use the author's supplied report-verification skill after review, with no claim that the skill reproduces FaithDetector or improves outcomes yet.

PERSPECTIVES

1. Practitioner
Criterion: understand what to do differently without adopting a new mandatory gate.
Questions: Which claim changes the next decision? What evidence supports it? Is the skill already in use or merely proposed?
Strength: the fixed claim and revised wording connect evidence to a concrete handoff. Skill is available verbatim.
Gap: future-tense summary and trial wording conflicted with the author's statement that the skill is in use.
Change: summary and introduction now describe current use; evaluation remains proposed and can justify changing or stopping use.
Limit: no observation of the author's real tasks or effectiveness of this skill.

2. Research-oriented reader
Criterion: reconstruct the selected findings and their boundaries.
Questions: Where did reports come from? What was labeled? What does the score measure?
Strength: separate denominators for reports and classified claims; disclosure preserves detailed evaluation scope and limits.
Gaps: sample provenance and taxonomy-selection context were missing; metric followed its score; checker criticism was only hypothetical.
Changes: identified SWE-chat/public-repository conversations and challenged/admitted taxonomy cases; explained annotation target and macro-F1 before scores; added the paper's lockfile false alarm.
Limit: checked the reported study, not replication data or a reproduction of its detector. Annotations are reference judgments, not universal truth.

3. Less experienced technical reader
Criterion: follow the argument without reconstructing the paper or knowing its vocabulary first.
Questions: Why did startup still fail? What changes between example states? What is FaithDetector checking?
Strength: concrete SQL-filter explanation; approved actions/evidence/claims passage; contrasting reports for each record.
Gap: undefined dataset and generic references to class scores could obscure what was evaluated.
Change: added local context and concrete questions about alarms against annotations. Kept the optional method depth separate from the main argument.
Limit: this is editorial inspection; no unfamiliar-reader comprehension check.

4. Presentation and interaction
Criterion: the previous essay's visual system supports reading and copying at multiple text sizes.
Questions: Do section links reach their targets? Can keyboard users inspect alternatives and copy? Does enlarged text reflow?
Strength: reused previous article's real stylesheet; numbered sections; native disclosures and download; full source remains readable without JavaScript.
Gap: download action overflowed at 200% text on a narrow viewport.
Change: allowed action text to wrap within its available width; linked evidence buttons to their controlled panels.
Limit: no full screen-reader journey; copy handler tested with a clipboard double rather than the operating-system clipboard.

SYNTHESIS AND CHANGES
Convergence: missing study context weakened both accessibility and evidence integrity. Fixing it required a few local sentences rather than a longer research-method section.
Complementarity: the observed checker false alarm supports the practical skill's distinction between overclaim and unavailable evidence.
Tension: more methodological detail improves auditability but costs reading time. Kept the task/metric definition visible and the full denominator and intervention limits in the existing disclosure.
Unique finding: enlarged-text overflow was a rendered layout defect, not an editorial concern.
Priorities addressed: research context; metric ordering and meaning; present-use/evaluation boundary; observed counterevidence; enlarged-text reflow.
Rejected/deferred: larger experiment, extra charts, mandatory reviewer gate, invented usage results, changes to the supplied skill, or a new site-wide style. No added interaction was needed for the argument.
