Solve the Constrained Problem

What solution works under the constraints that actually exist?

Use this when the theoretically correct solution is expensive, slow or incompatible with real constraints.

You’ll be able to state the real constraints and compare approaches that can work within them.

The idea

Real problems are defined partly by deadlines, latency, resources, incomplete data and other constraints. A theoretically imperfect approximation may solve the actual problem better.

Worked example · illustrative

Serving dinner with one oven

Situation. You are cooking for eight people with one oven and an hour before they arrive. Your preferred menu requires three dishes at different temperatures. One guest also has a food allergy.

The tempting conclusion. The best dinner is the full menu, so work faster to make it happen.

What changes the view. The oven schedule makes that menu infeasible within the time available. A cold starter, one baked main, and a ready-made dessert can meet the actual goal of feeding everyone well. The allergy remains a firm requirement; reducing complexity does not justify ignoring it.

What this supports. Choose a menu that works with the available time, equipment, and dietary needs. Accepting a less ambitious dessert may improve the whole meal. Check which constraints are real and which are preferences before compromising: a later serving time could change the options if the guests are comfortable with it.

Useful when

Planning under a deadlineWorking with limited resourcesBalancing requirementsChoosing a feasible first version

Watch for

“Pragmatic” shouldn't become an excuse for ignoring requirements that genuinely determine success. Sometimes complexity really is necessary.
