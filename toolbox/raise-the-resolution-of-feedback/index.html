Raise the Resolution of Feedback

Have I made the work concrete enough for someone to give me the kind of feedback I want?

Use this when feedback remains vague or high-level.

You’ll be able to give feedback about a specific moment, action, and possible improvement.

The idea

The quality of feedback is partly constrained by the resolution of the artifact being reviewed. Rough work often permits only structural feedback; clearer work exposes deeper issues.

Worked example · illustrative

Getting useful feedback on a workshop

Situation. You ask a colleague whether your proposed workshop sounds good. You describe the topic and receive only “seems useful.” You need feedback on whether participants will understand the central idea.

The tempting conclusion. The colleague needs to think harder or provide more detailed comments.

What changes the view. Your description gives them little evidence about the teaching. You share a short opening explanation and one sample activity instead. Now they can point to an unfamiliar term and a step where participants may get lost. The artifact exposes the issue you wanted reviewed without requiring a finished slide deck.

What this supports. Make the relevant part concrete enough for the feedback you need. Use a sample explanation to test understanding and a rough outline to discuss overall direction. More polish is not always more informative; complete visual design would add little to this particular review.

Useful when

Requesting feedback on a draftPlanning a workshopReviewing a proposalAsking for help with a specific concern

Watch for

Make the part relevant to the requested feedback concrete. Premature polish can waste effort or make tentative choices appear settled; identify what remains open.
