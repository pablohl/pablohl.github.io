Decision Toolbox

Questions that help you examine unclear decisions.

Start with a choice or difficulty you’re facing. Pick one question that seems relevant, read its example, and ask what it changes about your understanding or next step. You don’t need to use every tool.

Start with a situation

Find a situation close to yours. Start with the suggested tool; consider the alternative if its distinction fits your problem better.

  1. A result looks impressive, but I’m unsure whether to trust it.

    Start here: The Shape of the EvidenceCheck whether the surrounding observations fit the explanation.

    Also consider: What Did the Metric Throw Away?The headline number may hide differences that matter.

  2. I understand the findings, but I’m unsure what we should do.

    Start here: Decision-CompleteExamine the reasoning connecting the findings to a practical choice.

    Also consider: What Is Worth Learning?That reasoning reveals an unanswered question, and you need to decide what to investigate.

  3. We keep gathering information without getting closer to a decision.

    Start here: What Is Worth Learning?Identify which uncertainty could actually change the choice.

    Also consider: Two ClocksFurther learning may help, but you need to consider what waiting could cost.

  4. I’m unsure whether to act now or wait.

    Start here: Two ClocksConsider how information and available options change during the delay.

    Also consider: Position Before You KnowYou cannot commit yet, but could prepare in ways that help across plausible outcomes.

  5. Something feels wrong, but I can’t explain why.

    Start here: From Intuition to ArgumentIdentify what your concern is noticing and make the reasoning inspectable.

    Also consider: Look at the BoundaryThe concern involves different areas of expertise, and understanding their connection may reveal the problem.

  6. A detailed plan includes things we haven’t actually agreed on.

    Start here: Surface the Hidden DecisionsSeparate agreed commitments from assumptions and suggestions.

    Also consider: Change the RepresentationPeople agree on the wording but understand its scope or consequences differently.
