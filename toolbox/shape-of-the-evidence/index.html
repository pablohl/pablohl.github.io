The Shape of the Evidence

What would I expect to see around this result if my explanation were actually right?

Use this when one result looks substantially better than the alternatives.

You’ll be able to name the surrounding evidence that would make an unusually strong result more convincing.

The idea

A result is more convincing when the surrounding evidence has a structure consistent with the explanation. An isolated maximum may be real, but it demands explanation.

Worked example · illustrative

One unusually successful market day

Situation. A community group tests three prices for its fundraising lunch on different market days. The middle price brings in far more money than either of the others. The group must choose a price for its next event.

The tempting conclusion. The middle price clearly works best; use it again.

What changes the view. That day also coincided with a nearby festival. If the price attracted more buyers, you would expect stronger sales under comparable conditions too. Comparing attendance and sales per visitor, or repeating the price on an ordinary day, helps distinguish the price explanation from the extra crowd. The exceptional result needs an explanation beyond its size.

What this supports. Treat the middle price as a promising candidate, not an established winner. Seek surrounding evidence consistent with the explanation before planning revenue around it. A genuine price threshold is possible; an isolated result is a reason to investigate, not automatically reject it.

Useful when

Comparing unusually strong resultsEvaluating a successful trialChoosing between providersChecking whether an improvement is repeatable

Watch for

Real phenomena can have thresholds and discontinuities; smoothness is evidence, not proof.

Where this came from

Quantitative trading, Machine learning

Lineage

Skepticism about unusually good quantitative research results developed into examining the structure of surrounding evidence.
