Engineering Toolbox / Research decisions

What Does This Result Let Us Decide?

The experiment produced an answer. What action does that answer support?

01 / The problem

From a result to a recommendation

A report can contain accurate numbers, clear figures, and a section of limitations while leaving the reader to reconstruct the recommendation.

The missing connection may be an explanation, a decision criterion, or evidence that has not yet been collected. Identifying the gap tells us what work to do next.

02 / The distinction

Observation →then Interpretation →then Decision

Observation
What changed, compared with what, and on which cases?
Interpretation
What does that establish, and what remains uncertain?
Decision
Given our requirements, what should we do next?

A result supports an action only under the requirements and conditions that connect them.

03 / Explore an example

What changes the recommendation?

Two illustrative scenarios. Change one condition to see what it permits.

The model costs less. Does the work cost less?

You are deciding whether to replace the current model to reduce total operating cost. A cheaper model has similar automated evaluation scores on the same documents. Reviewers notice that some answers need more editing. Both options must meet the same quality requirements.

The tempting conclusion

“The scores are similar and the model costs less. We should switch.”

The evaluation supports a claim about measured performance and model cost. It does not yet tell us whether extra review effort offsets the saving.

Has the review-time comparison resolved the trade-off?

Resolve the missing evidence

Evidence: The model-cost result stays the same. Reviewer observations suggest a possible offsetting cost, but its size is unknown.

Recommendation: Keep the current model while comparing review time on paired outputs. Include review costs and check the same quality requirements before deciding whether to switch.

Why: The missing evidence concerns the outcome that motivated the change. More runs of the automated metric alone would not settle it.

Make a bounded switch

Evidence: In this branch, a paired comparison establishes lower total operating cost, including review, while meeting the required quality level on the evaluated work.

Recommendation: Switch for the evaluated document types, monitor review effort and quality, and revisit the choice if the production workload differs.

Why: The added evidence connects the lower model cost to the actual objective. It supports a scoped recommendation without establishing performance on every future document.

Identify what would settle the choice

A useful recommendation names both what we learned and the unresolved question that could change the choice. The next experiment should address that question.

How this could sound in the report
“The cheaper model has similar automated scores on the evaluated documents, but editing effort is unmeasured. I recommend a paired review-time trial before replacing the current model because our objective is lower total operating cost.”

A useful follow-up: Compare outputs for the same documents, measure review time consistently, and agree on the quality and cost requirements before inspecting the result.

The parser works on digital PDFs. Can we roll it out?

A document parser meets the agreed extraction-quality requirement on the evaluated digital PDFs. Scanned documents were not tested. You need to decide whether that missing coverage blocks the proposed rollout.

The tempting conclusion

“Scans were not tested, so we cannot deploy.”

The evidence has a boundary. Whether that boundary prevents deployment depends on what the proposed rollout includes and how excluded documents will be handled.

What must the proposed rollout handle?

Limit the rollout to supported inputs

Evidence: The evaluated digital PDFs meet the agreed quality requirement. Scan performance remains unknown.

Recommendation: Run a monitored pilot for supported digital PDFs. Reliably route scans to the existing process and state that they are outside the pilot.

Why: The caveat limits scope. This recommendation depends on being able to enforce that boundary; if routing is unreliable, resolve it first.

Resolve coverage before the full rollout

Evidence: The experimental result is unchanged: digital PDFs meet the requirement; scan performance remains unknown.

Recommendation: Hold the rollout that includes scans. Evaluate representative scanned PDFs against the same requirements, or establish an accepted fallback before including them.

Why: The missing evidence now concerns a required input. It blocks this scope of rollout, even though a digital-only pilot could still be considered.

Translate a limitation into its practical consequence

A caveat can change the scope, the next test, or the recommendation. Explain which one it changes rather than leaving the reader to infer whether the whole result should be trusted.

How this could sound in the report
“The parser meets our extraction-quality requirement on the evaluated digital PDFs. I recommend a digital-only pilot with scan routing to the existing process. We have not established scan quality, so this result does not support including scans.”

A useful follow-up: Check that the pilot can reliably identify and route excluded inputs. If scans must be included, evaluate a representative scan sample before expanding.

04 / Discussion

What this distinction helps us notice

Name the decision criterion.

Lower model cost does not establish lower total operating cost. Say which outcome the decision needs to improve.

Make the caveat change something.

Missing scan coverage can limit a rollout to digital PDFs. If scans are required, it becomes a gap to resolve before that rollout.

Identify the work that remains.

An unclear result needs analysis. An unclear connection needs explanation. An untested assumption needs evidence. A disputed trade-off needs a decision from the responsible person.

The structureApplied to the lower-cost model

The cheaper model has similar automated scores on the evaluated documents, but reviewers report more editing.

The measured model cost falls. We have not established whether extra editing offsets that saving.
03 / DecisionGiven our requirements, what should we do next?
Keep the current model while running a paired review-time comparison. Switch only if the cheaper option meets quality requirements and reduces total operating cost.

A sentence to take with you

On [evaluated cases], we observed [result] compared with [baseline]. Given [requirement], I recommend [action and scope]. We have not established [uncertainty]; this matters because [effect on the decision].

If a part is missing, decide whether you need analysis, an explanation, evidence, or agreement on a trade-off.

Keep the conclusion proportionate. A recommendation can be to continue, narrow the scope, retain the current approach, or collect specific evidence. State what the uncertainty permits or prevents.
