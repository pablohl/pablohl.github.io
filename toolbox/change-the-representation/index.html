Change the Representation

Is this actually small, or does it only look small in the representation I'm using?

Use this when two people describe the same change as radically different in size or complexity.

You’ll be able to restate a comparison so that different descriptions can be checked against the same facts.

The idea

Something can be small at one interface and large inside the system—or technically difficult while almost irrelevant to the user.

Worked example · illustrative

Moving a meeting by one hour

Situation. An organizer proposes moving a regular meeting from four to five. On the shared calendar, this looks like a small adjustment. One participant says it would be difficult and asks to keep the original time.

The tempting conclusion. It is only an hour; accommodating it should be straightforward.

What changes the view. The participant explains that the new finish time misses their usual train and overlaps with childcare pickup. The calendar represents the meeting alone. Their daily schedule shows the dependencies that make the change costly. Another participant with a flexible evening may genuinely experience it as a small adjustment.

What this supports. Compare the affected schedules before choosing a time. A shorter meeting or another day may meet the organizer’s goal with less disruption. The broader representation reveals consequences; it does not automatically mean this participant’s preference must decide the outcome.

Useful when

Estimating a proposed changeCoordinating schedulesDiscussing scopeComparing visible effort with downstream consequences

Watch for

A more complicated representation isn't automatically the more relevant one. Ask which representation matters for the decision.
