Look at the Boundary

What might be visible from the other domain that isn't visible from mine?

Use this when capable people from different specialties disagree or unexpectedly miss something.

You’ll be able to find an assumption or handoff between specialties that needs checking together.

The idea

Expertise changes which differences, dependencies and consequences appear important. The missing information may exist in the connection rather than inside either person's domain.

Worked example · illustrative

A reminder that arrives too late

Situation. A community center sends appointment reminders by text. Staff confirm that messages are delivered, but volunteers report that some visitors still arrive without a required document. Both teams think the other has missed something.

The tempting conclusion. The reminder is working, so visitors must not be reading it carefully.

What changes the view. The volunteers explain that visitors need several days to obtain the document. The scheduling team sends reminders the evening before because that works well for confirming attendance. Neither team lacks competence: one sees successful delivery, while the other sees the preparation needed before arrival. The connection between timing and preparation was never discussed.

What this supports. Walk through the visitor’s preparation with both teams and send document instructions early enough to use. Keep a later attendance reminder if useful. This addresses an identified mismatch; it does not establish that every missed document has the same cause.

Useful when

Coordinating people with different expertisePlanning handoffsInvestigating missed requirementsResolving disagreements about a shared task

Watch for

You do not need to replace another expert or defer to them automatically. Check your interpretation with the people involved; apparent fluency in their language is not proof that you understand their constraints.
